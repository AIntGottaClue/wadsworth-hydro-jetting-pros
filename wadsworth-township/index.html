Hydro jetting questions for Wadsworth Township homes

Outside the city limits, a home may be on a public sewer or on its own septic system. That one fact changes every drain question.

Sewer or septic?

The Medina County Health Department permits and inspects new and replacement household sewage treatment systems in the county. It also says that if a property can reach a sanitary sewer, the house must be connected and the septic system properly abandoned under a permit.

Before any line cleaning, find out which kind of system your home uses. Hydro jetting a house line is not the same as servicing a septic tank or its leach field.

Coal country and glacial soil

Coal was mined throughout the township, and mining was the area's largest business for nearly 50 years, according to the Main Street Wadsworth history. The county itself is covered by glacial till, lake deposits and outwash over bedrock, according to the ODNR groundwater pollution potential report for Medina County.

Soils like these hold water differently from place to place. That can matter for septic fields and for how long a basement stays wet after rain.

If you are on county sewer

The Medina County Sanitary Engineer says that after a backup, its staff can check the main during business hours and help find cleanouts and the length of the lateral. It suggests a plumber be ready to clear the full lateral from the house to the main.

Questions about drains in Wadsworth Township

Is my Wadsworth Township home on septic?

Many outlying homes are, but some are on public sewer. The Medina County Health Department keeps records, and it offers instructions on how to request them.

Does hydro jetting clean a septic tank?

No. It cleans house lines. Tank pumping and field repair are separate septic work.

Is a septic inspection required when I sell?

The county health department says it does not require one at sale, though lenders often do.
