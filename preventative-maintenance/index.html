Preventative drain care for Wadsworth homes

A little attention to the private line, the sump pump and the floor drain can head off the kind of backup that ruins a basement.

What the city suggests

The Wadsworth Sanitary Sewer Backup Handbook says some owners keep roots under control by having the line cleaned on a regular schedule. It also lists simple home checks for people who depend on a sump pump: pour water into it every month to make sure it runs and empties quickly, and clean the strainer on the suction side every six months.

These are small tasks that cost little time and tell you early if something has changed.

Keep storm water out of the sanitary line

The handbook says foundation drains, roof downspouts and sump pumps should not be connected to the sanitary sewer. In its words, those connections violate city ordinances and add to overloading during heavy rain.

If your house is older and has a basement but no sump pump, ask a qualified professional to trace where the foundation drain goes.

Know your line before you need it

Ask for a camera inspection when you buy a house or when drains slow down. A recording of the pipe today makes it easier to spot changes later.

Call 811 before you dig

The city's planning and zoning page reminds residents to call Ohio Utility Protection Service at 811 before any digging.

Questions about preventative maintenance

How often should a sewer line be cleaned?

There is no single rule. The city handbook says some owners clean as often as every six months and others only every few years, depending on roots and the pipe.

What is a backwater valve?

It is a valve in the sewer line that can close to stop sewage from flowing back into the house. The handbook notes that valves can jam with debris, so they should not be counted on for full protection.

Should I get an inspection before buying an older home?

A camera inspection can show the condition of the private line before you own it. It helps you plan for repairs or regular cleaning.
