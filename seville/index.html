A small village with long history

Seville was settled in 1816 and became a village in 1828. It is bordered by Rittman, Creston, Sterling, Westfield Center and Wadsworth, according to a Beacon Journal community profile.

Because the village is small, it is easy to assume every house shares one era of plumbing. In practice each property has its own repair history, which only an inspection shows.

What to check

Homes built before modern plastic pipe may have clay or iron lines that have been patched over time. A camera inspection shows how much of the original line remains and whether roots or scale are present.

The village operates a wastewater treatment plant, so many properties are likely on public sewer, but a specific address should be confirmed with the village.

Questions about drains in Seville

Is Seville on public sewer?

Many properties are, since the village has its own treatment plant, but a specific address should be confirmed with the village.

Do older homes in Seville have clay lines?

Some may. A camera inspection shows what material is in your line and its condition.

Should I tell the village about a sewer backup?

Yes. A backup can come from the public main, so it is worth reporting.
