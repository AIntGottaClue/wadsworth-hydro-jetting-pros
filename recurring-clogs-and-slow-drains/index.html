Recurring clogs and slow drains in Wadsworth homes

When one drain keeps clogging, the answer is often not at the drain. It can be a partly blocked line, a main that fills in heavy rain, or both.

Three places a backup can start

The Wadsworth Sanitary Sewer Backup Handbook lists three causes: a blockage in the private sewer lateral, a blockage in the public main, or an overloaded main during rainstorms. Only the first is the owner's to fix.

If a neighbor with a similar house did not back up and you did, the private line is a likelier suspect. If several neighbors are affected, the main may be the cause, and the city should hear about it.

Why age of house matters

The handbook says houses built before 1960 often had a foundation drain connected to the sanitary service, and that roof downspouts and area drains were sometimes connected too. Those connections send storm water into a system built for household wastewater, and the city says they violate its ordinances.

A hydro jetting visit cannot undo that. It can clear buildup that is making an already stressed line worse.

What to do when a drain keeps clogging

  1. Report a backup.Sewage in the basement should be reported to the city first, then to a qualified professional.
  2. Look at the line.A camera shows buildup, roots, offsets or standing water.
  3. Match the fix to the cause.Cleaning for buildup, repair for damage, and a conversation with the city if the main is involved.

Questions about recurring clogs and slow drains

Why does the same drain clog every few months?

Repeat clogs often point to buildup or damage farther down the line. A camera can show what is there so the next step fits the cause.

Could my backup be from the city sewer?

It could be. The handbook lists a blocked or overloaded public main as one cause. The city asks residents to report backups.

Can heavy rain cause my drains to back up?

It can. The handbook explains that storm water entering the sanitary sewer can overload it and push sewage into basements through the lowest drain.
