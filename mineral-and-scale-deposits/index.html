Mineral and scale deposits in older Wadsworth pipes

Scale builds slowly on the inside of a pipe, so a drain can lose capacity over years without anyone noticing a single bad day.

How scale builds

Minerals in water can leave a hard crust inside a drain line, especially in older iron or galvanized pipe and at spots where flow slows. The pipe still carries water, but through a smaller opening. Each new bit of grease or paper then has less room to pass.

Because the change is slow, people often blame the last thing that went down the drain. A camera can show the real condition of the pipe wall.

Older homes and mixed materials

Wadsworth's population grew quickly in the early 1900s when match, injector, salt and brick plants opened, and many of those houses are still in use, according to the Main Street Wadsworth history. Over a century, a house may have had pieces of its plumbing replaced while other pieces stayed.

A line of mixed materials can show scale in one stretch and clean plastic in the next. That is one reason to inspect before choosing a nozzle.

When cleaning is not enough

Hard scale can leave a pipe thin or pitted. If the camera shows a weak wall, repair may be the safer choice than pressure cleaning.

Questions about mineral and scale deposits

Can hydro jetting remove scale?

It can remove some deposits from suitable pipe. Hard scale on old, thin pipe may call for a gentler method or repair.

How do I know if scale is the problem?

Drains that slow gradually over a long time, in an older house, are a common pattern. A camera inspection can confirm it.

Is scale only a problem in old houses?

It is more common in older metal pipe, but any line can collect deposits over time.
